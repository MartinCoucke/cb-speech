Isabel Schnabel — ECB
Navigating stormy waters: reflections on monetary policy (2026-11-05)
In a valedictory speech at the ECB Conference on Money Markets, Isabel Schnabel outlines four lessons from her seven years on the Executive Board: preserve policy flexibility by avoiding over-commitment, respond to shocks based on their inflation implications rather than their type, protect monetary dominance from fiscal and financial risks, and implement monetary policy efficiently with a lean balance sheet. The speech is largely reflective and strategic rather than signaling an imminent rate change, though it notes that inflation expectations have drifted above the 2% target amid new overlapping shocks. Schnabel endorses the primacy of key policy rates and ongoing quantitative normalisation.
The speech is broadly balanced and retrospective, but Schnabel's emphasis on the risks of renewed inflationary pressures from overlapping supply shocks, drifting medium-term inflation expectations, the need to protect monetary dominance, and continued balance sheet normalisation leans slightly hawkish relative to a fully neutral baseline.
  • “In response to the latest energy shock, consumer inflation expectations over the medium to longer term have increased and gradually moved away from our 2% target.”
  • “We should be wary of commitments that depend too heavily on a particular economic environment or class of models, leaving little room to adjust when conditions change.”

Shaping the future of money (2026-11-05)
ECB Executive Board member Piero Cipollone delivered a keynote speech at a conference on regional card schemes and retail financial market infrastructures, focusing on the Eurosystem's comprehensive payments strategy. The speech covered four initiatives: modernising euro banknotes, developing a digital euro, supporting tokenised finance through the Pontes and Appia projects, and interlinking fast payment systems for cross-border payments. The speech contained no discussion of interest rates, inflation, or monetary policy stance.
This speech is entirely focused on payments infrastructure, digital currency development, and financial market innovation rather than monetary policy. There is no content relating to interest rate decisions, inflation outlooks, or easing/tightening bias.

Unknown speaker — Bank of England
Bank rate increased to 4% - November 2026 Monetary Policy Summary and Minutes (2026-11-05)
The Bank of England's MPC voted 8-1 to raise Bank Rate by 25 basis points to 4% at its November 2026 meeting, driven by surging energy prices linked to the Middle East conflict pushing UK CPI inflation to 3.5% and forecast to reach ~5% by mid-2027. The sole dissenter, Catherine Mann, preferred a larger 50bp hike on risk-management and credibility grounds. The Committee signalled it is not on a pre-set path but that further tightening may be warranted depending on the evolution of second-round effects, energy prices, and domestic wage and price dynamics.
The decision represents a clear hawkish action—a rate hike with a majority signalling openness to further tightening—but the Committee's emphasis on data-dependence, absence of second-round effects so far, and one member explicitly framing the move as a 'one-for-now' insurance hike tempers the hawkishness somewhat, placing it at a moderately hawkish rather than strongly hawkish reading.
  • “The duration and scale of the energy price increase had generated sufficient inflationary pressures in the pipeline to warrant an increase in Bank Rate.”
  • “Achieving the level of Bank Rate necessary to confront challenges and risks to lasting achievement of the 2% inflation target is likely to require more policy tightening, including in the near term.”

Trust, Expectations and Monetary Policy (2026-11-05)
Deputy Governor Andrew Hauser delivers a wide-ranging speech on the importance of public trust for central bank effectiveness, drawing on RBA survey data, economic models and historical context. He discusses how trust in the RBA's inflation-fighting credibility affects the efficacy and cost of monetary policy, and outlines the RBA's investments in research, modelling, data and communications to strengthen that trust. The speech contains no explicit forward guidance on the direction of interest rates or the balance of risks to the outlook.
The speech is focused on institutional trust and central bank credibility rather than signalling a particular policy direction; there is no lean toward easing or tightening, and no commentary on the current stance being too tight or too loose relative to the economic outlook.
  • “if the public understands and trusts the central bank's strategy, the policy response required to deliver a given disinflation can be smaller and less costly in terms of lost output”
  • “A reserve of trust is not a licence to disappoint. It makes the continuing work of earning trust more pressing, not less.”

Opening Statement to the Standing Senate Committee on Banking, Commerce and the Economy (2026-11-05)
Bank of Canada Governor Tiff Macklem testified before the Senate Banking Committee following a 25 basis point rate hike to 2.5%, the first increase since July 2023. The hike was driven by deteriorating inflation outlook stemming from elevated global oil prices and resilient economic growth despite US trade headwinds. CPI inflation is running around 3% and is projected to remain elevated through much of 2027 before returning to target by mid-2028.
The statement is moderately hawkish: the Bank has just resumed a tightening cycle after a pause, explicitly flagged upside inflation risks from energy prices and pass-through, and left the door open to further rate increases, while also signaling a cautious, meeting-by-meeting approach given elevated uncertainty.
  • “There could be a need to raise the policy rate further, but given the uncertainties, we will proceed carefully.”
  • “With elevated inflation expected to last longer and the economy showing resilience, Governing Council agreed that risks to inflation had increased and there was less scope for monetary accommodation.”
