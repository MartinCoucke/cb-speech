Michele Bullock — Reserve Bank of Australia
Opening Statement to the Senate Economics Legislation Committee (Supplementary Budget Estimates) (2026-10-28)
Governor Bullock delivered an opening statement noting that the RBA has raised the cash rate by a cumulative 100 basis points in 2026, with inflation running at 3.5–4% and risks skewed to the upside relative to August forecasts. She cited both persistent domestic capacity pressures and global cost shocks—particularly Middle East conflict-driven energy prices and the AI boom—as drivers keeping inflation elevated well above target. The Board is set to meet the following week to assess whether further tightening is needed.
The statement has a clearly hawkish tilt: the RBA has already tightened 100bp in 2026, inflation has re-accelerated and upside risks have materialised, and Bullock explicitly flagged that the sufficiency of current tightening remains an open question ahead of the next Board meeting, consistent with a bias toward further hikes.
  • “inflation picked up over the second half of 2025 and has remained above target since then...inflation has been around 3½ to 4 per cent in year-ended terms”
  • “A key question is whether the tightening in monetary policy to date will be sufficient to bring inflation back to target in a reasonable time”
