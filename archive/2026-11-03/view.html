Unknown speaker — Bank of Finland
The path back to 2 % has become less comfortable (2026-11-02)
Bank of Finland Board Member Tuomas Välimäki describes the euro area inflation outlook as 'less comfortable' following a renewed energy shock that has pushed headline inflation to 3.8%, while noting that underlying inflation, wages, and long-term expectations remain broadly stable. He explains the ECB's decision to hold rates unchanged at its October meeting, emphasizing a meeting-by-meeting, data-dependent approach given the stagflationary nature of the shock. He acknowledges upside inflation risks but stresses the need to distinguish between transitory headline effects and persistent inflation dynamics.
The speech is balanced, neither signaling imminent rate hikes nor cuts; the ECB held rates and Välimäki stresses caution and data dependence in both directions, with the energy shock creating offsetting pressures on inflation and growth that prevent a clear directional lean.
  • “the renewed energy shock has made the outlook more uncomfortable, but it has not – at least so far – fundamentally changed the underlying inflation picture”
  • “pre-committing to a rate path would create a false sense of precision”

Unknown speaker — Reserve Bank of Australia
Statement by the Monetary Policy Board: Monetary Policy Decision (2026-11-03)
The Reserve Bank of Australia's Monetary Policy Board raised the cash rate by 25 basis points to 4.85 per cent, citing elevated and broadening inflation that has been revised materially higher in staff forecasts. The Board noted upside risks to inflation expectations, with inflation now not expected to return to the top of the 2–3 per cent target range until late 2027, around three quarters later than previously forecast. The decision was passed by a majority of six to three members.
The decision to hike rates, combined with a materially higher inflation forecast, rising inflation expectations, and an explicit warning about embedding higher inflation expectations, firmly signals a hawkish stance. The Board's forward guidance emphasises continued attentiveness to upside inflation risks, though the minority vote and acknowledgment of slowing growth prevent a maximum hawkish score.
  • “Inflation is now expected to remain above 3 per cent for the coming year and there is an increasing risk that higher inflation expectations will become embedded.”
  • “Given the revised inflation outlook and the continuing upside risks, the Board judged that a further tightening in financial conditions is warranted to support the return of inflation to target.”
