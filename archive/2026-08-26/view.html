Perspectives on the Economy from Susan M. Collins (2026-08-25)
Boston Fed President Susan Collins characterizes the economy as expanding near trend with a broadly balanced labor market, but expresses significant concern about inflation that has remained above the 2% target for over five years. She supported the July FOMC decision to hold rates steady at a mildly restrictive level, while explicitly flagging upside inflation risks from supply shocks, tariffs, and AI-driven goods inflation. Notably, she signals openness to tightening policy if inflation progress fails to materialize.
Collins leans hawkish by emphasizing persistent above-target inflation, conditioning any rate hold on continued disinflation evidence, and explicitly stating that tightening 'will be appropriate soon' if progress stalls — a clear tightening bias rather than a neutral or easing posture.
  • “Should evidence of sustained inflation progress not materialize, I believe it will be appropriate to tighten policy soon to ensure we deliver price stability in a reasonable time frame.”
  • “With inflation still too high, I remain particularly concerned about the price stability portion of the dual mandate.”
