Federal Reserve issues FOMC statement (2026-10-28)
The FOMC voted 10-2 to hold the federal funds rate steady at 3¾–4%, with two dissents in favor of a 25bp hike. The statement notes strong economic activity, resilient domestic spending, robust capital investment, and elevated inflation, while signaling that more data will guide future policy decisions. The Committee reaffirmed its commitment to returning inflation to the 2% target.
The hold decision with two hawkish dissents, combined with language emphasizing elevated inflation and a strong economy, leans modestly hawkish. The statement's firm commitment to price stability and acknowledgment that rates may need to rise further tilts it above neutral.
  • “Inflation remains elevated. The accumulation of more information will help the Committee determine the policy stance appropriate to ensure a timely return of inflation to the Committee's 2 percent goal.”
  • “Voting against the monetary policy action were Lisa D. Cook and Beth M. Hammack, who preferred to raise the target range for the federal funds rate by 1/4 percentage point at this meeting.”

Christine Lagarde, Boris Vujčić: Monetary policy statement (2026-10-29)
The ECB Governing Council held all three key interest rates unchanged at its October 2026 meeting, citing a significant energy price shock that is pushing inflation well above target while simultaneously weighing on growth. Underlying inflation has remained broadly stable and wage pressures moderate, but upside risks to inflation dominate the risk assessment. The statement reaffirms a data-dependent, meeting-by-meeting approach with no pre-commitment to a rate path.
The hold decision combined with asymmetrically upside inflation risks, rising headline inflation (3.8%), a strong energy shock, and tightening financial conditions tilts the statement modestly hawkish relative to neutral, though the growth headwinds and lack of second-round effects prevent a more aggressive score.
  • “Energy prices have continued to rise, posing upside risks to the inflation outlook and downside risks to the growth outlook.”
  • “The energy shock could intensify further and its effects on other prices and wages could be stronger than currently expected.”

Release of the Monetary Policy Report (2026-10-28)
Bank of Canada Governor Macklem announced a 25 basis point rate hike to 2.5%, the first increase since July 2023, citing a deteriorated inflation outlook driven by persistently high global oil prices and improving economic resilience. CPI inflation is running around 3% and is projected to rise to ~3.5% by year-end, remaining above target through much of 2027. The Governing Council acknowledged elevated uncertainty from Middle East developments and US trade actions, signaling a cautious but potentially further tightening path.
The decision to hike after a prolonged pause, with inflation projected well above target and a warning that further rate increases may be needed, is clearly hawkish. However, the explicit 'one meeting at a time' guidance, acknowledgment of economic slack, and emphasis on elevated uncertainty temper the hawkishness to a moderate level.
  • “There could be a need to raise the policy rate further, but given the uncertainties, we will proceed carefully.”
  • “With elevated inflation expected to last longer and the economy showing resilience, Governing Council agreed that risks to inflation had increased and there was less scope for monetary accommodation.”
